# PR KOL Background Research

Use this skill to assess an individual creator, influencer, or creator account for PR outreach, product seeding, paid content, event participation, review, ambassador work, or an ongoing partnership. The user may provide a name, one or more account links, target market, brand/product, campaign objective, and possible cooperation type. Research the named person and their public professional content; do not assume that similarly named accounts belong to the same person.

If a missing detail would materially change the recommendation, such as target country, product category, audience, or campaign objective, ask a concise question while completing independent research where possible. State which parts of the recommendation depend on missing context.

When the user provides an activity or campaign purpose, make the final assessment specific to it. Explain whether the creator/account should be included, why the content and audience fit that purpose, what cooperation format to use, what angle or experience to offer, what to confirm before inviting, and which success measures are appropriate. Do not stop at a generic profile or fit score. If some campaign details remain unknown, make a practical recommendation using the stated purpose and label the assumptions.

## Research standards

- Start from the creator's official/public platform accounts. Confirm identity by matching links, handles, bios, and cross-links. Record the platform, account URL, displayed follower/subscriber count, and lookup date for each account. If the identity match is uncertain, keep accounts separate and explain the ambiguity.
- Use current public sources for dynamic facts. Prefer the creator's own account and content for account metrics and collaboration examples; use platform analytics, reputable measurement providers, brand/campaign pages, or original posts for audience and performance claims when available. Treat search snippets, reposts, and third-party influencer directories as leads, not sole proof for important claims.
- Attach a source link and publication date or measurement period to material claims. State the research cutoff date. Distinguish **verified facts**, **sample-based observations**, **inferences/recommendations**, and **items to verify**.
- Do not invent follower counts, audience demographics, engagement rates, reach, clicks, conversions, sponsorships, controversies, or account ownership. When a metric is unavailable, say so and describe the evidence needed to verify it.
- Treat cross-platform follower totals as a gross account footprint, not deduplicated people or guaranteed reach. Do not equate followers, views, impressions, unique reach, engagement, event attendance, clicks, or conversions.
- Audience authenticity tools and public signals are estimates. Do not label an account fraudulent based only on a tool score, one unusual post, or a single low-engagement period. State the signal, sample, method, limitations, and confidence; use “疑似异常/需进一步核实” unless reliable evidence supports a stronger conclusion.
- Limit personal information to public, professionally relevant material. For controversy or brand-safety concerns, provide context, date, source, the creator's public response if available, relevance to this campaign, and confidence. Do not infer private traits or make unsupported allegations.

## Research and assessment

1. **Cross-platform account footprint:** Find and verify the creator's relevant public accounts. Tabulate current followers/subscribers by platform with URL, metric type, lookup date, and source. Note inactive, region-specific, or uncertain accounts. Report the platform-by-platform footprint and, if useful, a clearly labeled gross total; never call it unique reach.
2. **Audience and account quality:** Assess audience geography, language, age or other audience profile only when supported by platform analytics or a credible, identified source. Review available authenticity indicators and engagement quality. Where analytics are unavailable, use a recent, disclosed sample of public posts and label conclusions as indicative. Examine whether commenters appear relevant and substantive, and whether interaction patterns are consistent; avoid claiming bot activity without evidence. Compare the creator's audience with the user's target market and campaign audience.
3. **Content verticals and style:** Review representative recent content across the relevant platforms. Summarize primary and secondary content categories, approximate share only when a defined sample supports it, formats, tone, production style, posting cadence, recurring themes, and audience interaction. Explain how the content connects to the brand or product and identify any mismatch.
4. **Effectiveness:** Assess effective reach, interaction quality, and audience fit using the best available evidence. Prefer platform-provided analytics or campaign reporting for reach, clicks, and conversions. If these are unavailable, use clearly labeled public proxies such as recent views and substantive comments, and say they do not establish unique reach or conversion. When calculating rates, show the formula, sample, period, and denominator; use comparable posts and avoid mixing platforms or formats. Describe clicks or conversions only when sourced campaign data supports them.
5. **Past commercial collaborations and recommendation:** Identify publicly documented brand partnerships, approximate timing, platform, format, frequency, disclosure, category overlap, and any observable audience response. Distinguish paid sponsorship, gifting/seeding, event attendance, affiliate, ambassador, and editorial/organic mentions where evidence permits; do not infer payment from a product mention alone. Recommend suitable cooperation formats for the current objective, explain why they fit, and list the deliverables or data to request before contracting. Do not promise results.
6. **Risks and decision:** Evaluate suspected fake followers or anomalous growth, low-quality interaction, audience mismatch, dated or active controversies, brand-safety concerns, over-commercialization, repeated endorsements, category conflicts, and excessive or weakly disclosed sponsorship. For each material risk, state evidence, date, severity, confidence, campaign relevance, and what would resolve uncertainty. End with an overall fit recommendation and the conditions or checks that could change it.
7. **Purpose-specific recommendation:** Translate the research into an action for the stated activity. For an automotive test-drive invitation, assess new-car review relevance, Australian-market and audience fit, hands-on testing capability, recent coverage of the brand/product/category, and any disclosed commercial relationship. Recommend an invitation priority and a suitable format (for example, independent test-drive access, a group drive, or separately labeled sponsored production), along with a proposed content angle, information/materials to prepare, any independence or disclosure boundary, and practical outcome measures. Keep editorial invitations separate from paid creator deliverables; an invitation does not guarantee coverage. Adapt these criteria to other campaign purposes instead of applying the test-drive checklist universally.

## Recommended report structure

Start with a short decision summary: **overall fit**, **recommendation for the stated activity**, **target-market/audience fit**, **strongest evidence**, **main risks**, and **key data gaps**. For multiple KOLs, compare them using the same criteria before writing individual profiles.

### 1. Account footprint

| Platform | Handle/account link | Followers/subscribers | Metric and lookup date | Source | Identity/status |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |

Add a cross-platform gross account total only if useful, with a note that audiences may overlap and the total is not unique reach.

### 2. Audience and account quality

| Platform | Audience geography/profile | Authenticity signals | Engagement quality | Evidence, period, and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Include the target audience comparison. If age, location, gender, or other demographics are not publicly verifiable, mark them “not publicly verified” rather than estimating.

### 3. Content verticals and style

Summarize main verticals, representative examples with links/dates, formats, tone, cadence, production style, and fit with the brand/product. State the sample size and time window. Do not present sample-derived category shares as full-account analytics.

### 4. Effectiveness evidence

| Platform/format | Metric | Value or observation | Period/sample | Source/method | What it does and does not show |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Cover effective reach, interaction quality, audience match, and—only when supported—clicks or conversions. Clearly separate measured outcomes from public proxies and estimates.

### 5. Past collaborations and recommended cooperation

| Brand/campaign | Date | Platform/format | Cooperation type and disclosure | Evidence/observable response | Relevance or conflict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Then recommend one or more formats (for example, product experience/review, short-form video, livestream, event attendance, seeding, affiliate, or ambassador collaboration) tied to the campaign objective. State suggested deliverables, information to request from the creator, and relevant category exclusivity or disclosure questions.

### 6. Risks, gaps, and recommendation

| Risk or gap | Evidence and date | Severity/confidence | Relevance to this campaign | Follow-up check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Close with a concise recommendation: **prioritize / consider / deprioritize / insufficient evidence**, plus the rationale and any conditions. When the user states an activity, include the recommended action, format, angle, preparation, and success measures for that activity. Do not turn uncertainty into a negative allegation.

## Multiple-creator comparison

When screening a list, use consistent criteria and separate market/audience fit, content fit, audience quality, performance evidence, cooperation suitability, and brand-safety risk. Explain any weighting or priority tiers. Do not rank creators solely by follower count or use a precise composite score when evidence is too uneven to support it.
